# Phase-Change Battery Explained

## Overview

A **phase-change battery** (PCM thermal battery) stores heat by melting and solidifying a special material. During this phase transition, the material absorbs or releases large amounts of energy at a constant temperature, enabling compact and stable thermal storage.

## How It Works

### Charging (Heat Absorption)

A heat source—typically a heat pump or PV-powered electric element—heats the phase-change material (PCM) to its melting point. As the PCM melts, it absorbs a large amount of **latent heat** without increasing in temperature.

### Storage

Once melted, the PCM remains in a liquid state as long as its temperature stays above the melting point. Because latent heat storage is highly efficient, PCM modules can store much more energy per liter than water.

### Discharging (Heat Release)

When heat is needed, the PCM cools and solidifies. During solidification, it releases the stored latent heat at a **constant temperature**, making it ideal for domestic hot water and low-temperature heating systems.

## Why PCM Is Better Than a Traditional Water Buffer

* **High energy density**: PCM stores 3–5× more energy per liter than water.
* **Constant output temperature**: PCM maintains a fixed temperature (e.g., 55°C) during discharge.
* **Compact design**: A 50-liter PCM module can replace a 200-liter water tank.
* **Perfect for PV integration**: Excess solar power can be converted into stored heat efficiently.
* **Reduced peak electrical load**: Heat pumps can operate when COP is highest, lowering grid demand.

## Typical Home Integration

A PCM battery is usually a compact module (30–80 liters) connected to a heat pump or electric heater. It provides thermal storage for domestic hot water or heating and can be controlled by smart home systems.

## Example: PCM at 55°C

* **Melting point**: 55°C
* **Storage capacity**: 3–6 kWh per 50 liters
* **Applications**: Domestic hot water, low-temperature heating

## Summary

A phase-change battery stores heat by melting and solidifying a special material, enabling compact, efficient, and stable thermal energy storage—ideal for modern heat pumps and solar-powered homes.
